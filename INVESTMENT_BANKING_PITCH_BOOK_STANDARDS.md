## Overview & Purpose

### What is an Investment Banking Pitch Book?

A pitch book is a **sales presentation** created by investment bankers to persuade clients or potential clients to hire their advisory services. Pitch books typically contain:
- Transaction merits and analysis
- Potential buyers/sellers
- Pricing and valuation information
- Key risks and mitigation strategies

### Typical Dimensions
- **Main Deck:** 10-20 slides (concise, focused)
- **Comprehensive Pitch:** 40-page slide deck with 60 pages of appendices
- **Format:** Printed, glossy, spiral-bound copies (historically)
- **Timeline:** 4-6 months typical deal process (can be as short as 3 months)

### Three Primary Types

1. **Market Overviews/Bank Introduction** (10-20 slides)
   - Introduce bank to prospective clients
   - Recent market activity
   - Firm credentials

2. **Sell-Side M&A Pitch Books** (100+ slides)
   - Company positioning for sale
   - Potential buyer profiles (longest section)
   - Valuation analyses
   - Exit recommendations

3. **Buy-Side Pitch Books**
   - Acquisition strategy framework
   - Target company profiles
   - Strategic fit analysis

## Standard Pitch Book Structure

### Situation-Complication-Hypothesis Framework

Almost all investment banking pitch books follow this narrative structure:

1. **Situation (Current State):** "Your prospective client is looking for growth"
2. **Complication (Problem):** "The potential client's growth rate has been slowing"
3. **Hypothesis (Solution):** "Acquiring a growing company can meet the client's need for growth"

### Five Main Sections

#### Section I: Company Overview
- Client company profile
- History and mission
- Financial performance snapshot
- Strategic positioning
- **One concept per slide**

#### Section II: Industry Overview
- Market trends and dynamics
- Sector developments
- Transaction activity
- Competitive landscape
- Charts illustrating current market conditions

#### Section III: Valuation
- **12-20+ slides** (varies by pitch complexity)
- Football field valuation chart
- DCF model output
- Comparable public companies
- Historical/precedent transactions
- Sensitivity analyses

#### Section IV: Transaction Opportunities
- Strategic alternatives
- Potential buyers/sellers list
- Recommended strategy
- Transaction timeline
- Process milestones

#### Section V: Team Overview
- Credentials and league tables
- Individual biographies
- Relevant deal experience
- Education backgrounds
- Why this bank is the right choice

### Additional Standard Sections

**Cover Page**
- Investment bank's name and logo
- Client's name and logo
- Date
- Clean and professional aesthetic

**Table of Contents / Index**
- All sections and subsections
- Page numbers
- Enables quick navigation

**Executive Summary**
- Concise overview of key points
- Purpose of the pitch
- Main recommendations
- Expected outcomes
- **One powerful slide, minimal text**

**Market Update**
- Charts on current capital markets conditions
- Convince clients timing is right for transaction

**Deal Outline**
- What the deal would involve
- Alternative options to consider

**Considerations**
- Charts/tables showing effects on EPS
- Potential risks and problems
- **READ THE FOOTNOTES** - risks often buried here
- References to "execution" or "management" style

**Appendix**
- Financial modeling assumptions/details
- Backup information
- Models with variety of scenarios
- **"Most important section despite placement at end"**

## Typography & Font Standards

### Primary Fonts Used

**Serif Fonts (Most Common):**
- **Garamond** - Used by Lazard, Evercore
  - Size: **10pt-12pt** for body text
  - Size: **11pt** common for financial modeling
  - Conveys sophistication and authority
  - Classic, scholarly vibe for formal contexts

- **Times New Roman**
  - Quintessential professional serif
  - Facilitates reading comprehension
  - Ideal for dense financial data

**Sans-Serif Fonts:**
- **Arial** - Standard for tombstones and tables
  - Maintains easy readability
  - Allows clean conveyance of large data volumes

- **Helvetica** - Alternative professional choice

### Font Size Hierarchy

**For Presentation Slides:**
- **Titles:** 24pt - 32pt
- **Subtitles/Headers:** 18pt - 24pt
- **Body Text:** 12pt - 14pt
- **Table/Chart Data:** 10pt - 12pt
- **Footnotes:** 8pt - 10pt

**For Documents/Books:**
- **Body Text:** 10pt - 12pt
- **Headings:** 14pt - 18pt
- **Chapter Titles:** 18pt+

### Typography Best Practices

1. **Maximum 2 font families** per pitch book
2. **Consistency across all slides** - same fonts, sizes, styles
3. **Serif for formal sections** (credentials, analysis)
4. **Sans-serif for tables/data** (clarity and density)
5. **Avoid italic overuse** - Garamond italics can look odd
6. **Create visual hierarchy** through size progression (32pt → 24pt → 14pt)
7. **Bold for emphasis** on key figures and headers

## Color Schemes & Design

### Standard Investment Banking Color Palettes

#### Navy Blue Foundation
- **Primary Use:** Headers, key points, firm branding
- **Hex Examples:** #002855 (Navy), #004080 (Deep Blue)
- **Psychology:** Trust, reliability, professionalism, stability
- **Application:** Conservative tone without appearing overly corporate

#### Grayscale Elements
- **Dark Gray Text:** #2C2C2C or #333333 (looks nicer than pure black)
- **Light Gray Backgrounds:** #F5F5F5, #E8E8E8
- **Mid-Gray Accents:** #808080, #CCCCCC (Gray-50%, Gray-25%)
- **Benefits:**
  - Sharp contrast for readability
  - Polished, professional look
  - Accessible for color vision deficiencies
  - **Contrast ratio minimum: 4.5:1**

#### Accent Colors
- **Dark Red/Maroon:** #8B0000 - Firm colors, emphasis
- **Dark Orange:** #CC5500 - Highlighting data points
- **Dark Green:** #006400 - Positive metrics, growth
- **Plum/Purple:** #663399 - Alternative accent
- **Gold/Copper/Champagne:** #B8860B - Sophistication, metallic accents

### Recommended Color Combinations

**Conservative Banking Palette:**
```
Text Dark 1:    Black (#000000)
Text Light 1:   White (#FFFFFF)
Text Dark 2:    Dark Red (#8B0000)
Text Light 2:   White (#FFFFFF)
Accent 1:       Dark Red (#8B0000)
Accent 2:       Gray-50% (#808080)
Accent 3:       Dark Blue (#00246B)
Accent 4:       Gray-25% (#BFBFBF)
Accent 5:       Navy (#000080)
Accent 6:       Plum (#663399)
```

**Modern Sophisticated Palette:**
- Base: Charcoal (#36454F), Navy (#000080), Black (#000000)
- Metallic Accents: Gold (#B8860B), Copper (#B87333), Champagne (#F7E7CE)

**Navy + Light Gray Classic:**
- Navy Blue: #002855
- Light Gray: #E8E8E8
- White: #FFFFFF
- Dark Gray: #333333

### Color Application Rules

1. **5-6 colors maximum** - avoid clutter
2. **High contrast required** (minimum 4.5:1 ratio)
3. **Test for color blindness** compatibility
4. **Align with firm brand colors** while prioritizing clarity
5. **Use color strategically** to:
   - Emphasize key points
   - Differentiate categories
   - Create hierarchy
   - Guide the eye

6. **Avoid:**
   - Too many bright colors (looks messy)
   - "Festival of colors" approach
   - Purple gradients and cliched patterns
   - Low contrast combinations

## Number Formatting Conventions

### Currency Abbreviations

**Standard Conventions:**
- **Millions:** `$MM` or `$M` (Latin: Mille = thousand, MM = thousand thousand)
  - Example: `$150MM` or `$150M`
  - Oil & Gas uses `mm` for millions, `m` for thousands

- **Billions:** `$B` or `$Bn`
  - Example: `$2.5B`

- **Thousands:** `$K` or `$k`
  - Example: `$500K`

**Alternative Notation:**
- `$100M`, `$100B`, `$100k`
- `Mn` or `Bn` for plain numbers
- Add currency symbol for monetary values to indicate both currency and specificity

### Excel Custom Number Formatting

**Display as Millions:**
```
#,##0,,"M"
$#,##0.0,,"M"
```
- Two commas after last digit

**Display as Billions:**
```
#,,,,"B"
$#,##0.0,,,"B"
```
- Three commas after last digit

**Display as Thousands:**
```
#,,"K"
$#,##0,"K"
```
- One comma after last digit

### Decimal Places by Metric

**Low-Value Multiples (more precision needed):**
- **EV/Sales, P/E/G:** `0.00x` (two decimal places)
- **Example:** `1.25x`, `0.87x`

**High-Value Multiples:**
- **EV/EBITDA, P/E:** `0.0x` (one decimal place)
- **Example:** `12.5x`, `18.3x`

### Negative Number Display

**Accounting Convention (Preferred):**
- Use **parentheses** instead of minus signs
- Example: `($2.5MM)` not `-$2.5MM`
- Excel format: `$#,##0.00;($#,##0.00)`

### General Number Formatting

**Thousands Separator:**
- Always use commas: `1,250,000` not `1250000`

**Currency in Headers:**
- Include unit in column header to reduce clutter
- Example: "Revenue ($ Millions)" or "All figures in millions of USD unless otherwise stated"
- Cleaner than formatting each cell with `$`

**Consistency Rules:**
1. Use **one standard format** throughout the workbook (number, currency, accounting)
2. **Same currency, units, and formats** across entire pitch book
3. **Match units in tables** - don't mix millions and billions in same table
4. Reduces confusion and errors

## Page Layout & Spacing

### Alignment Standards

**Critical Importance:**
- Investment bankers spend **up to 1/3 of workweek** creating/editing presentations
- **Proper alignment = professional credibility**
- All shapes and elements must be correctly aligned on each slide

### Types of Alignment

1. **Vertical Alignment** - Top, middle, or bottom of slide
2. **Horizontal Alignment** - Left, center, or right
3. **Central Alignment** - Centers lined up vertically or horizontally

**Best Practices by Element Type:**
- **Data tables:** Align vertically
- **Charts and graphs:** Align horizontally
- **Critical metrics:** Align centrally

### Grid and Guides

**PowerPoint Grid Settings:**
- Default: 1-inch squares (horizontal and vertical gridlines)
- Enable: View tab → Gridlines checkbox (Alt+W+S)
- Adjust spacing for precision alignment
- **Snap shapes to gridlines** for perfect positioning

**Smart Guides:**
- Automatic alignment suggestions as you drag objects
- Visual cues for consistent spacing

### Distribution and Spacing

**Even Distribution:**
- Select shapes → Shape Format → Align → Distribute Horizontally/Vertically
- PowerPoint automatically adjusts spacing to be equal
- Critical for data points on graphs, timeline markers

**Spacing Consistency:**
- Maintain uniform margins across all slides
- Standard practice: **0.5" - 1.0" margins** on all sides
- Leave adequate whitespace (35%+ recommended in general presentation design, though IB tends to be denser)

### Quick Access Toolbar (QAT) Setup

**Recommended Functions for Efficiency:**
1. Insert shape
2. Align object
3. Font color
4. Font size
5. Bring forward
6. Send backward
7. Eye dropper (color matching)
8. Merge shapes
9. Shape fill
10. Shape outline weight

**Time Savings:** Shaves seconds × thousands of repetitions from workload

## Slide-by-Slide Breakdown

### Cover Slide
**Components:**
- Investment bank name and logo
- Client company name and logo (if applicable)
- Presentation title
- Date
- Confidentiality disclaimer (if needed)

**Design:**
- Clean, professional aesthetic
- Generous whitespace
- Firm brand colors
- Large, clear typography

---

### Table of Contents
**Purpose:** Enable quick navigation in 40+ page decks

**Format:**
- Section names with page numbers
- Clear hierarchy (main sections vs. subsections)
- Optional: Brief description of each section
- Aligned left or centered

---

### Executive Summary Slide
**Structure:**
- **Company Overview** - Who you are, financial sector focus
- **Market Opportunity** - High-level demand insight, industry gap
- **Investment Thesis** - Core rationale (1-2 sentences)
- **Key Metrics** - Bold figures (revenue, market size)
- **Ask/Recommendation** - Clear action item

**Design Rules:**
- **Minimal text, maximum impact**
- No lengthy paragraphs
- Strong headings and bold figures
- Infographics & data points for key metrics
- Professional banking palette (blue, green, neutral tones)
- **One powerful slide only**

**Avoid:**
- Too much detail (belongs in later slides)
- Overuse of buzzwords without proof
- Lack of clear investment ask
- Weak visual hierarchy

---

### Market Update / Industry Overview Slides
**Content:**
- Charts illustrating current capital markets conditions
- Industry trends and sector developments
- Transaction activity (volume, value)
- Competitive landscape
- Why NOW is the right time for transaction

**Visuals:**
- Line charts for trends over time
- Bar charts for comparisons
- Market size graphics
- Growth trajectory illustrations

**Word Count:** Keep concise, 1 main point per slide

### Credentials / Team Slide
**Components:**

**League Tables:**
- Bank's ranking vs. competitors
- Rankings by: Deal Value, Deal Volume, Average Deal Size, Geography, Industry
- Data sources: Dealogic, LSEG, Bloomberg
- Format: Clean table or bar chart showing top 10 rankings
- Highlight your bank's position

**Team Biographies:**
- Name, title, photo
- Years of experience
- Relevant education (MBA, CFA)
- Notable deal experience (2-3 examples)
- Areas of expertise
- **Brief** - 3-5 bullet points maximum per person

**Firm Credentials:**
- Global presence map
- Firm history (brief)
- Key statistics (# of deals, AUM, etc.)
- **Focus on 2-3 relatable logos** showing similar experience
- Better to go deep than overwhelm with tangential experiences

### Transaction Tombstones
**Layout Options:**

**Option 1: Table Format (6 columns)**
```
| Date | Client | Description | Size | Industry | Role |
```
- Client and industry logos in designated columns or to the left
- Logos resized for visual balance while preserving legibility

**Option 2: Shape-Based Format**
- Rectangles with text boxes
- Copy, paste, and replace to build out natively in PowerPoint

**Typography:**
- **Sans-serif fonts (Arial)** for readability
- Standard fonts convey large amounts of critical data cleanly

**Content Per Tombstone:**
- Deal closure month/year
- Buyer and seller names
- Transaction headline (1 line)
- Total deal value (formatted: `$XXX MM`)
- Industry sector
- Bank's advisory role ("Exclusive Financial Advisor to...", "Lead M&A Advisor to...")
- Company descriptions (one line each)

**Design:**
- Use bank brand colors
- Simple and subtle design flourishes
- Maintain professional credibility
- Color formatting for small stylistic elements and backgrounds

**Hierarchy & Positioning (for co-advisors):**
- Lead underwriter/bookrunner: Upper left position
- Co-lead managers: To the right
- Other firms: Brackets grouped by underwriting risk, alphabetical order

### Timeline / Process Slide
**Purpose:** Show deal milestones and expected completion dates

**Standard Milestones:**
1. **Investment Teaser** - Anonymous 1-page summary
2. **NDA Exchange** - Confidentiality agreement
3. **CIM Distribution** - Confidential Information Memorandum
4. **Process Letter** - Timeline and contact info
5. **Management Presentations** - Site visits, Q&A
6. **IOI Submission** - Indication of Interest (preliminary non-binding offer)
7. **LOI Submission** - Letter of Intent (specific price, terms)
8. **Exclusivity Period** - ~30 days, pause other conversations
9. **Due Diligence** - Financial statement review, verification
10. **Final Documentation** - Legal documents, regulatory approval
11. **Closing** - Transaction completion

**Timeline Format:**
- Horizontal timeline with milestones marked
- Gantt chart showing phases
- Progress bars indicating current stage
- Date ranges for each phase
- Visual elements: arrows, milestone markers

**Typical Duration:** 4-6 months total (can be 3 months minimum)

- Organic growth vs. M&A
- Capital raising alternatives
- Pros/cons of each option
- Recommended strategy highlighted

**Format:**
- Table comparing alternatives
- Decision matrix
- Visual flowchart showing decision tree

## Valuation Pages

### Football Field Valuation Chart

**Purpose:**
- Visual summary of all valuation analyses
- Side-by-side comparison of methodologies
- Sanity-check different approaches against each other
- Demonstrate valuation range to client

**Name Origin:** Bars look like yard lines on a football field

**Standard Methodologies Included:**
1. **DCF Valuation** (Discounted Cash Flow)
2. **LBO Analysis** (Leveraged Buyout)
3. **Comparable Company Analysis** (Trading Comps)
4. **Precedent Transactions** (M&A Comps)
5. **52-Week Trading Range** (if public)
6. **Equity Research Estimates** (if available)
7. **Liquidation Analysis** (optional)
8. **Sum of the Parts** (optional)

**Chart Structure:**

**Y-Axis (Vertical, Left Side):**
- Lists valuation methods and assumptions
- Text aligned left
- Method name labels

**X-Axis (Horizontal, Bottom):**
- Unit of measurement for value
- **Public Companies:** Share price ($)
- **Private Companies:** Enterprise Value ($MM or $B)
- Scale from low to high

**Bars (Horizontal):**
- Each methodology = one horizontal bar
- Bar spans from low to high of valuation range
- Midpoint or mean often marked with vertical line
- Current share price shown as dotted line (if public)
- Acquisition value range highlighted (rectangle or bold box)

**Excel Implementation:**
- Use stacked bar chart
- "Minimum value" series with no fill (invisible)
- "Difference" series visible (creates floating bar effect)
- Highlight data (excluding method column) → Insert → Stacked Column
- Format minimum series: Fill = No Fill

**Color Conventions:**
- One color per methodology (e.g., different shades of blue/gray)
- Highlighted range: Bold color or outlined box
- Current price line: Dotted or dashed, contrasting color

**Benefits:**
- Visual comparison vs. table of numbers
- Quickly identify outliers
- See when methodologies cluster vs. diverge
- Average and midpoints visible at a glance
- Clean, uncluttered presentation

### Comparable Companies Analysis (Comps) Table

**Purpose:**
- Relative valuation methodology
- Compare target to similar public companies
- Derive valuation multiples from peer group

**Key Steps:**

**1. Select Comparable Companies**
- Same industry or sub-sector
- Similar geography
- Similar size (market cap, revenue)
- Similar business model
- Similar growth profile

**2. Gather Financial Data**
- Market Cap from stock price × shares outstanding
- Enterprise Value = Market Cap + Debt - Cash
- Historical financials: Revenue, EBITDA, EBIT, Net Income
- **Sources:** SEC filings (10-K, 10-Q), Bloomberg, Capital IQ, PitchBook
- Projected financials: Management estimates, sell-side analyst consensus

**3. Calendarize Data**
- Adjust for different fiscal year ends
- Ensure apples-to-apples comparison across peer group

**Table Format:**

**Columns (Left to Right):**
```
Company Name | Market Cap | Net Debt | Enterprise Value | Revenue | EBITDA | EBIT | EV/Revenue | EV/EBITDA | EV/EBIT | P/E
```

**Additional Columns (Optional):**
- Equity Value
- Free Cash Flow
- EV/FCF multiple
- Price/Book (P/B)
- Price/Cash Flow

**Rows:**
- List of comparable companies (alphabetical or by size)
- **Summary Statistics Row:**
  - High
  - Low
  - Mean
  - **Median (most meaningful - screens outliers naturally)**

- **Target Company Row** (highlighted or bolded)

**Formatting Best Practices:**

**Decimal Places:**
- **Low multiples (EV/Sales, P/E/G):** `0.00x` (two decimals)
- **High multiples (EV/EBITDA, P/E):** `0.0x` (one decimal)
- **Currency/Financials:** `$1,234.5` (millions)

**Separation:**
- Clearly separate market data, financial data, and multiples into sections
- Use horizontal lines or shading to distinguish section breaks
- Reader can easily follow information flow

**Color Coding:**
- Header row: Dark background (navy/gray), white text
- Data rows: Alternating white/light gray for readability
- Target company: Highlighted background (light blue/yellow)
- Summary stats: Bold border or shaded

**Alignment:**
- Company names: Left aligned
- Numbers: Right aligned
- Headers: Centered or left aligned

**Enterprise Value vs. Equity Multiples:**

**Operating Multiples (Enterprise Value-based):**
- EV/Sales, EV/EBITDA, EV/EBIT, EV/FCF
- Ignore financial leverage (Debt)
- Ignore Depreciation & Amortization
- Value total company vs. equity only
- **Most commonly used: EV/Sales, EV/EBITDA**

**Equity Multiples:**
- P/E, P/B, P/Cash Flow
- Ignore cash flow to debt holders
- Frequently used by equity analysts

**Matching Rule:**
- **Numerator → Value Measure** (Enterprise Value or Equity Value)
- **Denominator → Value Driver** (EBITDA, EBIT, Net Income, EPS)
- **Investor group must match on numerator and denominator**

**Application to Target:**
1. Calculate median (or mean) multiple from peer group
2. Apply to target's financial metric
3. **Example:** Median EV/EBITDA = 9.0x, Target EBITDA = $120MM
4. **Implied Enterprise Value = 9.0x × $120MM = $1,080MM**

### DCF Model Output Slide

**Purpose:**
- Intrinsic valuation methodology
- Present discounted cash flow analysis results
- Show sensitivity to key assumptions

**Key Components:**

**1. Base Case Valuation**
- Enterprise Value or Equity Value per share
- Key assumptions stated clearly:
  - WACC (Weighted Average Cost of Capital)
  - Terminal growth rate (perpetuity method)
  - OR Terminal multiple (exit multiple method)

**2. Terminal Value Methodology**

**Option A: Perpetuity Growth Method**
```
Terminal Value = FCFn+1 / (WACC - g)
```
- g = long-term growth rate

**Option B: Exit Multiple Method**
```
Terminal Value = EBITDAn × Terminal EV/EBITDA Multiple
```
- Look at current trading multiple or peer group average
- Common approach: Use current EV/EBITDA or peer average

**Dual Calculation:**
- Compute perpetuity growth rate implied by terminal multiple method
- Compute terminal multiple implied by perpetuity growth method
- Show both for cross-validation

**3. Sensitivity Table (Critical)**

**Two-Way Sensitivity Table:**
- **Rows:** Terminal growth rate (or terminal multiple)
- **Columns:** WACC (discount rate)
- **Cells:** Resulting enterprise value or share price

**Range Guidelines:**
- **WACC:** Vary by ± 0.5% to 1.0% (e.g., 8.0% to 10.0%)
- **Terminal Growth:** Vary by ± 0.5% to 1.0% (e.g., 2.0% to 4.0%)
- **Avoid wide ranges** - too speculative

**Table Format:**
- **Top-left cell:** Reference to output (e.g., Enterprise Value)
- **Lowest value:** Top-left of table
- **Highest value:** Bottom-right of table
- **Color gradient:** Light to dark (or white to blue) showing value progression
- **Base case cell:** Highlighted (bold border or different color)

**Example Layout:**
```
Terminal Growth →     2.0%    2.5%    3.0%    3.5%    4.0%
WACC ↓
8.0%                 $1,200  $1,250  $1,300  $1,350  $1,400
8.5%                 $1,150  $1,200  $1,250  $1,300  $1,350
9.0%                 $1,100  $1,150  $1,200  $1,250  $1,300  ← Base Case
9.5%                 $1,050  $1,100  $1,150  $1,200  $1,250
10.0%                $1,000  $1,050  $1,100  $1,150  $1,200
```

**Key Insights:**
- **1% change in WACC** can result in **15% valuation difference**
- Sensitivity analysis provides **valuation range** not single point
- Minor adjustments significantly alter results - handle with care

**4. Supporting Information**
- Free Cash Flow projections (5-10 years)
- Key assumptions table (revenue growth, margins, capex, etc.)
- WACC calculation breakdown
- Beta, risk-free rate, market risk premium, debt/equity mix

**Presentation Context:**
- DCF output almost always presented with **range of assumptions**
- Shown in context of other methodologies (comps, precedents)
- **Football field chart** is common presentation vehicle

**3-Variable Sensitivity (Advanced):**
- Innovative solution for analyzing three inputs simultaneously
- More complex Excel setup
- Less common in standard pitch books

### Precedent Transactions Analysis

**Purpose:**
- Historical M&A transaction comparables
- Show multiples paid in past deals
- Contextualize current valuation

**Table Structure:**
- Similar to Comps table
- Columns: Date, Target, Acquirer, Deal Value, Revenue, EBITDA, EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, Premium %
- Rows: Historical transactions (typically last 2-5 years)
- Summary statistics: High, Low, Mean, Median

**Key Differences from Trading Comps:**
- Multiples typically higher (acquisition premium included)
- Reflects control premium and synergies
- Date/vintage matters (market conditions change)

## Data Visualization Guidelines

### Choosing the Right Chart Type

**Trends Over Time:**
- **Line chart** - Single or multiple series
- **Area chart** - Emphasize magnitude of change
- **Use for:** Revenue growth, stock price trends, market size evolution

**Comparisons:**
- **Bar chart (horizontal)** - Compare categories
- **Column chart (vertical)** - Compare values across categories
- **Use for:** Company comparisons, regional performance

**Proportions/Percentages:**
- **Pie chart** - Show parts of whole (limit to 5-6 slices)
- **Donut chart** - Modern alternative to pie
- **Use for:** Market share, revenue mix, capital structure

**Relationships/Correlations:**
- **Scatter plot** - Show correlation between two variables
- **Bubble chart** - Add third dimension (size)
- **Use for:** Risk/return profiles, valuation multiples vs. growth

**Distributions/Ranges:**
- **Histogram** - Frequency distribution
- **Box plot** - Show quartiles and outliers
- **Use for:** Valuation ranges, trading multiples distribution

**Process/Timeline:**
- **Gantt chart** - Project timeline with phases
- **Horizontal timeline** - Sequential milestones
- **Use for:** Deal process, integration timeline

### Design Best Practices

**1. Accuracy and Professionalism**
- Avoid misleading scales or distorted axes
- Use clear, accurate labels
- Avoid decorative clutter
- Ensure data integrity

**2. Consistency**
- Same currency, units, and formats throughout
- Same colors, fonts, and styles for all charts
- Maintain visual coherence across pitch book

**3. Color Application**
- Use colors to emphasize, differentiate, and categorize
- Follow color theory principles:
  - **Complementary colors** for contrast (blue/orange)
  - **Analogous colors** for harmony (blues/greens)
  - **Darker colors for foreground**, lighter for background
- Avoid too many or too bright colors (looks messy)
- Test for color blindness accessibility

**4. Typography for Charts**
- **Sans-serif fonts** for titles and labels (Arial, Helvetica)
- Clear, legible font sizes (10pt minimum for readability)
- Consistent font sizing across all charts

**5. Simplicity and Clarity**
- One main idea per chart
- Remove unnecessary gridlines, borders, tick marks
- Maximize signal-to-noise ratio
- Amplification through simplification

**6. Strategic Use of Visuals**
- Use visuals to enhance message, not distract
- Complement text-heavy sections
- Make complex data digestible
- Data visualization > page of numbers (higher impact)

### Chart Formatting (Excel/PowerPoint)

**Chart Templates:**
- Create standardized chart template
- Apply to all charts for consistent look
- Format once, reuse across Word, Excel, PowerPoint
- Saves hours vs. manual formatting

**Common Formatting:**
- Remove chart border
- Subtle gridlines (light gray, thin)
- Clear axis labels (units specified)
- Legend positioned for clarity (top-right or bottom)
- Data labels where helpful (on bars, line endpoints)

**Animation (Use Sparingly in IB):**
- Investment banking presentations are for discussion, not standalone viewing
- Animated visuals can enhance engagement if used in live presentation
- **Keep animations minimal and professional**
- Focus on content, not flashy effects

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Lack of Clarity and Focus
- Pitch book should NOT be generic or overly comprehensive
- Must be **tailored to specific client and transaction**
- Avoid information that doesn't directly support the pitch

### 2. Information Overload
- Bombarding client with excessive numbers and complex charts
- Makes pitch hard to digest
- **Solution:** One concept per slide, use appendix for backup

### 3. Confusing Pitch Decks with Pitch Books
- **Pitch deck:** Visual guide to the story you tell (live presentation)
- **Pitch book:** Organized reference manual (distributed document)
- Don't try to present the book like reading an owner's manual

### 4. Accuracy and Proofreading Errors
- Zero tolerance for errors and typos
- Include latest company and industry information
- No analytical or typographical errors
- **Use:** Spell checker, grammar checker, proofreading software
- **Why:** Errors negatively impact credibility

### 5. Formatting and Design Inconsistencies
- Slides and charts must be consistent, clear, easy to read
- **Use:** Style guide, color scheme, font standards
- **Uniform color palette** across all slides
- **Headlines matter** - uniform formatting, drive messaging
- Inconsistency causes confusion regardless of convention chosen

### 6. Outdated or Inaccurate Data
- All details must be accurate and up-to-date
- No scope for mistakes that negatively impact client
- Information should be brief and to the point
- **Double-check every detail** before presenting
- Demonstrates professionalism and reliability

### 7. Not Tailoring to the Audience
- Competence likely not in question if you reached in-person pitch
- **Focus on what audience is looking for:**
  - 2-3 relatable logos showing similar experience
  - Go deep on similar projects
  - Don't overwhelm with tangential experiences

### 8. Poor Source Selection
- Preferred sources (in order of credibility):
  1. World-renowned organizations (WTO, World Bank, OECD)
  2. Recognized trade union research
  3. Research by educational institutes
  4. Big 3 consulting firm reports (McKinsey, BCG, Bain)
  5. Big 4 research reports (Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG)
- **Always corroborate data** from one source with another

### 9. Poor Use of Visuals
- Too many chart types in same presentation
- Inappropriate color choices or overuse
- Weak visual hierarchy (everything same size/weight)
- **Solution:** Consistent chart types, strategic color use, clear hierarchy

### 10. Weak or Missing Executive Summary
- Should be sharp, visually structured, persuasive
- Avoid lengthy overviews or excessive detail
- Include clear investment ask with amount and purpose
- No buzzwords without proof

### 11. Inadequate Appendix
- Failing to include supporting materials and backup
- Not anticipating questions with prepared backup slides
- **Solution:** Robust appendix with models, assumptions, scenarios

### 12. Failing to Read Footnotes
- Potential risks often buried in footnotes
- Referenced as "execution" or "management" style issues
- **Always review footnotes carefully** - critical information hiding there

## Appendix Standards

### Purpose
- Contains backup information the bank anticipates questions on
- Doesn't belong in main pitch book (keeps main deck focused)
- **"Most important section despite placement at end"**

### What to Include

**Financial Modeling Details:**
- Detailed assumptions for DCF, LBO, comps
- Sensitivity analyses with wider ranges
- Alternative scenarios (base, upside, downside)
- Full financial statement projections

**Supporting Materials:**
- Detailed financial models
- Supporting charts and graphs
- Relevant market research reports
- Industry reports and studies
- Regulatory filings excerpts

**Additional Analyses:**
- Alternative valuation methodologies not in main deck
- Detailed competitor profiles
- Extended transaction precedents list
- Credit analysis and metrics
- Accretion/dilution scenarios

### Format Standards

**Length:**
- Typical: **60 pages of appendices** for 40-page main deck
- Can be longer than main presentation

**Organization:**
- Numbered or lettered sections corresponding to main deck
- Table of contents for appendix (if very long)
- Clear page headers indicating appendix status

**Slide Formatting:**
- Same design standards as main deck
- Maintain consistency in fonts, colors, layouts
- May be slightly denser (more data per slide acceptable)
- Page numbers continue from main deck or restart (A1, A2...)

**Best Practices:**
1. **Keep main deck concise (10-20 slides)** - use appendix for backup
2. **Anticipate questions** - include supporting data for expected inquiries
3. **Cross-reference** - footnote in main deck when detail is in appendix
4. **Organize logically** - group related backup materials together
5. **Update with main deck** - ensure appendix reflects current data

## Summary Checklist for Automated Systems

### Typography
- [ ] Garamond 10-12pt for body text (or Times New Roman)
- [ ] Arial/Helvetica for tables and tombstones
- [ ] Maximum 2 font families
- [ ] Titles: 24-32pt, Subtitles: 18-24pt, Body: 12-14pt
- [ ] Footnotes: 8-10pt

### Colors
- [ ] Navy blue (#002855) for headers
- [ ] Dark gray (#333333) for text
- [ ] Light gray (#E8E8E8) for backgrounds
- [ ] 5-6 colors maximum
- [ ] Contrast ratio minimum 4.5:1
- [ ] Accent colors: dark red, dark orange, dark green

### Numbers
- [ ] Currency: `$MM` for millions, `$B` for billions
- [ ] Negative numbers in parentheses: `($2.5MM)`
- [ ] Decimals: `0.00x` for low multiples, `0.0x` for high
- [ ] Thousands separator: always use commas
- [ ] Units in headers: "All figures in $ millions"

### Layout
- [ ] Perfect alignment using grid and guides
- [ ] 0.5-1.0" margins on all sides
- [ ] Even distribution of elements
- [ ] One concept per slide
- [ ] 35%+ whitespace (where possible, though IB tends denser)

### Slide Structure
- [ ] Cover page: Bank logo, client logo, date
- [ ] Table of contents for 40+ page decks
- [ ] Executive summary: 1 powerful slide, minimal text
- [ ] Section headers for five main sections
- [ ] Page numbers and footers consistent
- [ ] Appendix clearly marked

### Valuation Pages
- [ ] Football field: All methodologies side-by-side
- [ ] Comps table: Market data | Financial data | Multiples (separated)
- [ ] DCF: Base case + 2-way sensitivity table
- [ ] Sensitivity: Low value top-left, high value bottom-right
- [ ] All valuations show range, not single point

### Data Visualization
- [ ] Right chart type for data (line for trends, bar for comparisons, etc.)
- [ ] Consistent chart template across all slides
- [ ] Clear labels and axes
- [ ] Avoid misleading scales
- [ ] Strategic use of color
- [ ] Minimal gridlines and clutter

### Quality Control
- [ ] No typos or errors
- [ ] All data up-to-date
- [ ] Sources credible (World Bank, Big 3 consulting, etc.)
- [ ] Footnotes included where needed
- [ ] Cross-references to appendix accurate
- [ ] Consistent formatting throughout
