# TED Talk & Keynote Presentation Best Practices - Comprehensive Research

This document synthesizes research from leading presentation experts and analyzes the structure of the world's most-watched TED talks. It provides actionable frameworks, slide-by-slide templates, timing breakdowns, and design principles for creating world-class keynote presentations.

## PART 1: EXPERT METHODOLOGIES

### 1. Nancy Duarte - The Sparkline Framework

#### Core Concept: "What Is" vs "What Could Be"
The Sparkline model creates contrast between current reality and desired future, oscillating between these two states to build tension and desire for change.

**How It Works:**
1. Present the status quo ("What Is") - current challenges, pain points, risks
2. Contrast with idealised future ("What Could Be") - vision, benefits, solutions
3. Repeat this pattern throughout the presentation
4. Build to climactic call-to-action

**Implementation Steps:**
- **Start by making the audience the hero** - Begin by describing specific challenges your audience faces using their language. Interview stakeholders beforehand to understand frustrations and goals.
- **Toggle between states** - Deliberately alternate between pain points and solutions throughout (don't group all problems at beginning and solutions at end).
- **End with a clear choice** - Frame a specific decision point. Make the call-to-action concrete and immediate (24-48 hours).

**Famous Example:** Martin Luther King Jr.'s "I Have a Dream" speech
- Paints vivid picture of racial discrimination (status quo)
- Juxtaposes with dream of harmonious society (ideal future)
- Creates compelling narrative structure that drives message home

**Presentation Timing Guidelines:**
- 36-90 hours to create a 30-slide, one-hour presentation
- Use time markers: "When I'm 1/4 through, I should be on this slide"
- Create mental checkpoints to track timing during delivery

**The 3-Second Glance Test:**
Slides should function like billboards - audience should grasp meaning in 3 seconds or less before turning back to presenter.

**Word Count Rule:**
- **Maximum 75 words per slide** - Beyond this, it becomes a document, not a presentation
- Either reduce content and put details in notes, or admit it's a document

#### STAR Moments - "Something They'll Always Remember"

**Purpose:** Create memorable points that leave lasting impressions and make your message resonate long after presentation ends.

**Ask yourself:** "When someone leaves my talk, what do I want them to talk about?"

**How to Create:**
- Memorable dramatization
- Repeatable sound bites
- Evocative visuals
- Emotive storytelling
- Shocking statistics

**Famous Example:** Steve Jobs (2008)
"This is the MacBook Air, so thin it even fits inside one of those envelopes you see floating around the office." Then pulled MacBook Air from manila envelope.

**Important:** Know your audience - don't create overly emotional moments for technical audiences like biochemists. Spend creative energy thinking of out-of-the-box ways to make points stick while avoiding cheesy or cliché solutions.

**Neuroscience Connection:**
Jaw-dropping moments create "emotionally charged events" - heightened emotional states that make audiences more likely to remember your message and act on it. The amygdala releases more dopamine during these moments, aiding memory processing.

### 2. Garr Reynolds - Presentation Zen

#### Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR)

**Definition:** The ratio of relevant to irrelevant elements in a slide or display.

**Core Principle:**
- Signal = your message
- Noise = everything else (clutter)
- Goal: Highest possible SNR

**Why It Matters:**
People have hard time coping with too much information. Get rid of anything that doesn't advance your message.

#### Amplification Through Simplification

**Core Message:** "By stripping down an image to essential meaning, an artist can amplify that meaning."

**Approach:**
- Lots of empty space to highlight one or few important elements
- Japanese Zen arts philosophy: Express great beauty and powerful messages through simplification
- Emotional impact of visual elements can significantly contribute to meaning

**Important Nuance:**
Not all non-essential elements constitute "noise" - Reynolds challenges blind application of SNR. What constitutes noise may vary among audience members. Use SNR as guiding principle, not absolute rule.

**Reality Check:**
Simplicity is not easy. This approach will NOT save you time. But it will make your presentations meaningful, powerful, and memorable.

### 3. Chris Anderson - TED Talks: The Official TED Guide

#### The Throughline Concept

**Definition:** The unifying thread running through a talk, connecting various elements and guiding audience understanding.

**Core Idea:** All great movies, plays, and novels have a throughline - your presentation needs one too.

**How to Develop:**
1. Determine your point/theme
2. Express in ONE sentence (15 words maximum)
3. Ensure robust content - not just "inspire audience" or "win support"
4. Avoid predictable throughlines

**Example:**
- Weak: "I want to talk about my trip to Alaska and how Inuits live"
- Strong Throughline: "People who live in small, self-sufficient communities live more creatively than people in big cities"

**Provocation Creates Interest:**
Which sounds more interesting?
- "The Dangers of Procrastination" (predictable)
- "The Benefits of Procrastination" (element of surprise)

**Concept Limit:** Your throughline can't connect large number of concepts. Stick with 1-3 concepts maximum for talk to land.

**Requirements for Worth Saying:**
1. Show why it matters
2. Flesh out with memorable examples or stories

**Examples from Popular TED Talks:**
- "More choice actually makes us less happy"
- "Vulnerability is something to be treasured, not hidden from"
- "Education's potential is transformed if you focus on amazing creativity of kids"
- "A history of universe in 18 minutes shows path from chaos to order"

#### The 18-Minute Strategy

**Why 18 Minutes?**
TED discovered most humans can focus on interesting topic for ~20 minutes without much effort, even unfamiliar with field. Natural curiosity carries them through 20 minutes, but not 60 minutes unless truly interested in topic.

**Purpose:** Short talks allow anyone regardless of background to enjoy them.

**Timing Best Practice:**
Time yourself. Practice with clock winding down in front of you.

#### Opening Hooks

**Goal:** Give audience reason to step away from comfort zone and accompany you on journey of discovery.

**It's OK to:** Save big revelations for middle or end. In opening sentences, sole goal is hooking attention.

**Proven Opening Techniques:**

1. **Startling Statement**
   - Example: "The image you're about to see changed my life"
   - Example: "I'm going to play you a video that, at first viewing, may seem impossible"

2. **Provocative Question**
   - Example: "Here's my opening slide. Can you figure out what this thing is?"

3. **Unexpected Reveal**
   - David Christian's history of universe: Opened with video of egg being scrambled, then revealed it was happening in reverse (unscrambling). Revealed throughline: there is direction to time.

4. **Time-Based Mystery**
   - "Until two and a half months ago, no living human had cast eyes on this object"

### 4. Carmine Gallo - Talk Like TED

#### The 9 Public Speaking Secrets

After analyzing 500 presentations, Gallo identified common elements that make TED Talks successful.

**Three Components of Inspiring Presentation:**
1. **Emotional** - Touch the heart
2. **Novel** - Teach something new
3. **Memorable** - Present content in unforgettable ways

#### The Rule of Three

**Core Principle:** Structure presentation in three main points.

**Why It Works:**
- If you want audience to remember your talk, keep it short
- Cover no more than three themes
- Appeal to audience's senses

#### Emotional Connection (Pathos)

**The Mix:** Analysis of hundreds of TED talks revealed most popular presentations consist of:
- **65% Pathos** (emotional connection)
- 25% Logos (logic/data)
- 10% Ethos (credibility)

**Key Insight:** Pathos is THE most essential feature of persuasive presentation.

**Best Method:** Storytelling
- Makes presentation less abstract
- Makes content more identifiable
- Helps connect with audience

**First Three Secrets (Emotional Connection):**
1. Releasing the master within
2. The art of storytelling
3. Having a conversation

#### Jaw-Dropping Moments

**Definition:** Emotionally charged events creating heightened state of emotion that makes audiences more likely to remember message and act on it.

**Famous Example:** Bill Gates brought jar of mosquitos to TED talk about malaria dangers.

**Neuroscience:** Amygdala releases more dopamine when exposed to jaw-dropping moments, aiding memory processing.

**Requirement:** Every presentation needs one of these moments.

### 5. Chip & Dan Heath - Made to Stick: The SUCCESs Framework

**Based on 10 years of study** answering: Why do some ideas succeed while others die?

#### The Six Principles

**S - Simple**
- Distill complex, multifaceted ideas into single core idea
- Easy to understand
- Can be embodied in compact plain-English phrase/sentence

**U - Unexpected**
- Get attention through emotion of Surprise
- Keep attention through emotion of Interest

**C - Concrete**
- Concrete ideas easier to understand and remember than abstract concepts
- When people can visualize and relate to idea, it becomes more memorable

**C - Credible**
- Crucial for making ideas believable and convincing
- When supported by credible evidence and authoritative sources, audience more likely to trust and accept

**E - Emotional**
- For people to take action, they first have to care
- Focus on individual (not masses)
- Use associations
- Appeal to self-interest
- Appeal to identity

**S - Stories**
- Provide context and meaning
- Help audience connect with message on personal level
- Make abstract ideas concrete
- More memorable than dry facts
- Inspire action by illustrating real-life application

#### The Curse of Knowledge

**Problem:** Once we know something, we have difficulty imagining what it's like to not know it.

**Result:** We struggle to recreate learners' state of mind, making ideas hard to stick.

**Solution:** Consciously work to remember your audience's perspective and knowledge level.

## PART 2: TOP TED TALKS STRUCTURAL ANALYSIS

### Talk #1: Simon Sinek - "Start With Why" (60M+ views)

#### Opening Technique
**Rhetorical Questions** - Engaged audience immediately with series of questions about leadership and inspiration.

**First Line Territory:** "How do great leaders inspire action?"

#### The Golden Circle Framework (Core Structure)

**Three Concentric Rings (Inside Out):**

1. **WHY (Inner Ring)**
   - Purpose, cause, or belief
   - Few know this
   - Not just profit

2. **HOW (Middle Ring)**
   - The process
   - Some businesses know this

3. **WHAT (Outer Ring)**
   - What you do
   - Every business knows this

**Key Insight:** Most organizations communicate outside-in (What → How → Why). Great leaders communicate inside-out (Why → How → What).

**Recurring Refrain:** "People don't buy what you do; they buy why you do it"

#### Story Structure

**Setup:** Re-emphasized Apple as brand, flag bearer of product innovation. Discussed how Macintosh (1984) and iPod (2001) changed the world.

**Examples Used:**
- Apple
- Martin Luther King Jr.
- Wright Brothers

**Core Question:** What makes a leader inspirational?

#### Neuroscience Connection
Successfully communicating the passion behind the "Why" communicates with listener's limbic brain - part that processes feelings like trust and loyalty, as well as decision-making.

#### Key Ideas Presented
1. In Golden Circle, "why" is more important than "what" or "how" for driving human behavior
2. People do things to prove what they believe
3. People are drawn to leaders who share their values and beliefs

#### Visual Approach
- Simple concentric circles diagram
- Minimal text
- Clear visual metaphor

#### Closing Technique
Reinforced central message: Start with Why to inspire action and loyalty.

#### Word Count Estimates
- **Opening (0-2 min):** ~250-300 words - Posed central question
- **Middle (2-16 min):** ~1,750-2,000 words - Explained Golden Circle with examples
- **Close (16-18 min):** ~250-300 words - Reinforced "Start With Why"

**Total:** ~2,250-2,600 words (125-145 WPM)

### Talk #2: Brené Brown - "The Power of Vulnerability" (71M+ views)

#### Opening Technique
**Personal Story + Self-Deprecation** - Shared anecdote about academic colleague calling her "storyteller" (not meant as compliment).

**Immediate Connection:** Revealed own vulnerability in opening, building strong audience connection.

**Credibility Established:** Introduced research background while maintaining relatability.

#### Story Structure

**Opening (0-2 min):**
- Personal anecdote establishing identity as "researcher-storyteller"
- Promise: "I will tell stories about research that fundamentally expanded my perception and changed the way I live, love, work, and parent"

**Key Themes Developed:**
1. Shame is universal experience
2. Courage and self-compassion allow us to embrace vulnerability
3. Vulnerability leads to joy and meaningful connections

**Middle Section - Research Findings:**
- Used data from qualitative study (grounded theory methodology)
- Created models reflective of real-world complexities
- Told stories constructed from actual research data

**Emotional Turning Point:**
Powerful message on emotional numbing: "You can't numb those hard feelings without numbing the other affects, our emotions. You cannot selectively numb. So when we numb those, we numb joy, we numb gratitude, we numb happiness."

**Closing:**
Emphasized embracing vulnerability to live fulfilling lives with wholeheartedness.

#### Presentation Approach
- Described herself as "researcher-storyteller"
- Committed to empirical evidence
- Constructed narratives from data, not just anecdotes
- Balance of research credibility with emotional storytelling

#### Visual Aid Approach
- Minimal slides
- Focus on speaker and story
- Data presented simply when needed

#### Word Count Estimates
- **Opening (0-2 min):** ~250-300 words - Personal story + setup
- **Middle (2-18 min):** ~2,000-2,250 words - Research findings + emotional insights
- **Close (18-20 min):** ~250-300 words - Call to embrace vulnerability

**Total:** ~2,500-2,850 words (~125-142 WPM)

### Talk #3: Ken Robinson - "Do Schools Kill Creativity?" (76M+ views)

**Most-watched TED Talk of all time**

#### Humor Integration

**Style:** Loose, relaxed, less earnest than modern talks. Truly laugh-out-loud funny.

**British Delivery:**
- Deadpan
- Self-deprecating
- Intentionally finding jokes, making them look unintentional and incidental

**Impact:** Laughter disarms audience, making them more receptive to critique of education and open to change.

**Stats:** Robinson elicited laugh from audience throughout talk maintaining engagement.

#### Structure and Storytelling

**Opening Hook:**
Used humor immediately to establish rapport and tone.

**Stories with Unexpected Punchlines:**
Creates element of surprise throughout talk.

**Famous Humorous Anecdote - Nativity Play:**
- First boy: "I bring you gold"
- Second boy: "I bring you Mer"
- Third boy: "Frank sent this"

**Another Key Story:**
Successful dancer who has had powerful cultural influence - grounded ideas and helped audience build emotional connection to topic.

**Story of Misunderstood Four-Year-Old:**
In drawing lesson - humanized problem, made arguments relatable and unforgettable.

#### Use of Pauses
Understood how pauses help command room as speaker. Created nice tension. Audience constantly anticipating what comes next.

#### Key Arguments

**Central Argument:**
Creativity is as important as literacy, but education system doesn't value it. In fact, we're educating people out of creative capacities.

**The Hierarchy Problem:**
Education systems everywhere share same hierarchy:
1. Math and languages (top)
2. Humanities (middle)
3. Arts (bottom)
   - Even within arts: Music/visual arts valued more than drama/dance

**Root Cause:** Hierarchy rooted in Industrial Age where education designed to produce workers for economy that no longer exists.

**Future Focus:** Creativity is crucial 21st century skill needed to solve today's pressing problems. But by adulthood, most kids have lost that capacity.

#### Visual Approach
- Minimal slides
- Focus on speaker's personality and delivery
- Humor carried more weight than visuals

#### Word Count Estimates
- **Opening (0-2 min):** ~250-300 words - Humorous setup
- **Middle (2-17 min):** ~1,875-2,125 words - Stories + arguments with humor
- **Close (17-19 min):** ~250-300 words - Call for education system makeover

**Total:** ~2,375-2,725 words (~125-143 WPM)

#### Legacy
Robinson (1950-2020) was renowned British educator, author, speaker. Professional work had significant impact on education reform worldwide.

**Key Lesson:** Humor can be incorporated into presentation to strengthen audience engagement.

### Talk #4: Amy Cuddy - "Your Body Language May Shape Who You Are" (71M+ views)

**Second most-watched TED Talk of all time**

#### Personal Story Structure

**Background Story:**
Amy Cuddy wasn't supposed to become successful scientist. Early in college career, suffered severe head injury in car accident. Doctors said she would struggle to fully regain mental capacity and finish undergraduate degree.

**The Turnaround:**
She proved them wrong. With support from doctoral advisor Susan Fiske, learned to "fake it till she became it" with postural awareness as key part of success.

**Story Integration:**
Presentation offered compelling evidence along with moving personal narrative - brilliant student who had very bad car accident resulting in significant brain injury, who then had to relearn everything in re-orienting herself in academic world.

#### Opening Technique

**Interactive Start:** Asked audience to do quick assessment of their body posture.

**Visual Examples:** Showed images and videos of politicians' body language.

#### Key Ideas Presented

**Key Idea 1:** Body language affects how others perceive us and can influence hiring decisions and election outcomes.

**Key Idea 2:** "Power poses" are proven to work (Note: Later research showed mixed replication results).

**Key Idea 3:** We can use body language to change our perceptions of ourselves and our abilities.

#### Presentation Techniques

**Storytelling:** Shared personal anecdotes from research career, adding sense of humanity to scientific data.

**Visual Aids:** Clear and simple graphs and photos illustrating statistics and scientific evidence.

**The Science:**
- Power posing: Adopting open, expansive postures conveying confidence
- Contrast with "low-power poses" (closed and contractive)
- Original research suggested 2 minutes of power posing could:
  - Increase testosterone
  - Decrease cortisol (stress hormone)
  - Lead to feeling more confident and less stressed

#### Structure Breakdown

**Opening (0-2 min):** Posture assessment + introduction
**Middle (2-18 min):** Research findings + personal story
**Close (18-21 min):** "Fake it till you become it" message

#### Visual Approach
- Clear scientific charts
- Photos demonstrating poses
- Before/after comparisons
- Simple, clean design

#### Word Count Estimates
- **Opening (0-2 min):** ~250-300 words
- **Middle (2-18 min):** ~2,000-2,250 words
- **Close (18-21 min):** ~375-425 words

**Total:** ~2,625-2,975 words (~125-142 WPM)

#### Controversy Note
By 2016, co-author Dana Carney decided she did not "believe that 'power pose' effects are real." Some studies failed to replicate hormone findings, but other studies did find beneficial impacts from expansive posture.

### Talk #5: Bill Gates - "The Next Outbreak? We're Not Ready" (2015)

#### Prop Usage (STAR Moment)

**The Barrel Entrance:**
Gates entered stage while wheeling in large army-green barrel reminiscent of wartime.

**Curiosity Created:** Piqued curiosity, posed single question in minds of audience: "What is in there?"

**The Reveal:**
"When I was a kid, the disaster we worried about most was a nuclear war. That's why we had a barrel like this down in our basement, filled with cans of food and water. When the nuclear attack came, we were supposed to go downstairs, hunker down, and eat out of that barrel."

**Purpose:** Drew parallel between Cold War preparedness and lack of pandemic preparedness.

**Note:** In later TED appearance, Gates practiced bringing Roman fire brigade bucket as prop, noting "(It's a lot heavier than it looks!)"

#### Talk Structure

**Opening:**
Vivid story of global health crisis that killed millions. Warning that another epidemic inevitable but we are not ready.

**Key Idea 1:** Likelihood of highly infectious virus emerging is high.

**Key Idea 2:** World's healthcare systems lack preparedness for epidemic. Critical need for investment in research and infrastructure.

**Key Idea 3:** We must take threat of next epidemic seriously.

**Closing:** Emphasized importance of preventing future global health crisis. Called on governments, private sector, and individuals to work together to:
- Improve disease surveillance
- Develop new vaccines and treatments
- Invest in research

#### Presentation Techniques

**Storytelling:** Took audience back to 2013 Ebola outbreak, vividly painting picture of "global failure."

**Humor:** Shared Hollywood's depiction of global health crises and contrasted with reality to emphasize world doesn't take threats seriously enough.

#### Key Recommendations Structure

**First:** Strong health systems in poor countries
- Where mothers can give birth safely
- Kids get all vaccines
- Early outbreak detection

**Second:** Medical reserve corps
- People with training and background ready to go
- Paired with military
- Taking advantage of military's ability to move fast, do logistics, secure areas

**Third:** Simulations
- Germ games, not war games
- See where the holes are

#### Visual Aids Approach
- Data visualizations showing outbreak patterns
- Maps of disease spread
- Simple charts
- Prop (barrel) as memorable anchor

#### Word Count Estimates
- **Opening (0-1.5 min):** ~187-225 words - Barrel prop + setup
- **Middle (1.5-7.5 min):** ~750-900 words - Problem + recommendations
- **Close (7.5-8.5 min):** ~125-150 words - Call to action

**Total:** ~1,062-1,275 words (~125-150 WPM for 8.5 min talk)

## PART 3: SLIDE-BY-SLIDE KEYNOTE TEMPLATES

### Template 1: The Sparkline Structure (18-Minute TED Format)

**Total Slides: 15-20 slides**
**Speaking Pace: 125-150 WPM**
**Total Words: ~2,250-2,700 words**

#### OPENING SEQUENCE (2 minutes, 250-300 words)

**Slide 1: Title Slide (10 seconds)**
- ONE powerful image
- Presentation title (6 words max)
- Your name
- NO date, NO corporate logo clutter
- Design: 60% whitespace minimum

**Slide 2: The Hook (30-45 seconds)**
Choose ONE:
- Startling statement with striking visual
- Provocative question (large text, one line)
- Unexpected statistic (number dominates slide)
- Personal story opening (one evocative image)

**Slide 3: Set Context (45-60 seconds)**
- Where we are today
- ONE image showing current reality
- 6-10 words maximum
- Establish "What Is"

**Slide 4: Why This Matters (30 seconds)**
- Stakes/impact
- Simple visual metaphor
- 5-8 words

#### MIDDLE SEQUENCE - THE OSCILLATION (12-14 minutes, 1,500-1,750 words)

**The Pattern: Alternate between "What Is" and "What Could Be"**

**Slide 5: Problem #1 - Current Reality (60 seconds)**
- Specific pain point
- Dark or muted imagery
- 8-12 words
- Make audience feel the problem

**Slide 6: Solution #1 - Better Future (60 seconds)**
- Corresponding solution
- Bright, optimistic imagery
- 8-12 words
- Show contrast

**Slide 7: Problem #2 - Current Reality (60 seconds)**
- Different angle on challenge
- Visual consistency with Slide 5 aesthetic
- 8-12 words

**Slide 8: Solution #2 - Better Future (60 seconds)**
- Second solution element
- Visual consistency with Slide 6 aesthetic
- 8-12 words

**Slide 9: Data/Evidence Slide (60-90 seconds)**
- ONE chart or graph
- Remove all non-essential elements
- Highlight ONE data point
- Label in 42pt+ font
- Zero words in bullets

**Slide 10: STAR Moment (60-90 seconds)**
- Your "Something They'll Always Remember"
- Could be:
  - Shocking statistic (number fills slide)
  - Powerful image (full bleed)
  - Demo or prop moment (minimal or no slide)
  - Jaw-dropping revelation
- Maximum emotional impact
- This is the slide people will screenshot

**Slide 11: Story Slide (90-120 seconds)**
- Personal narrative or case study
- ONE image that anchors story
- Maybe one short quote (15 words max)
- Let your voice carry the story

**Slide 12: Problem #3 - The Stakes (60 seconds)**
- What happens if we don't change
- Powerful imagery
- Create urgency
- 6-10 words

**Slide 13: Solution #3 - The Vision (60 seconds)**
- Ideal future state
- Most optimistic imagery yet
- 6-10 words
- Build toward climax

#### CLOSING SEQUENCE (3-4 minutes, 375-500 words)

**Slide 14: The Gap (45 seconds)**
- Visual showing distance between current and future
- Could be:
  - Before/after split screen
  - Journey metaphor
  - Bridge metaphor
- Minimal text (5 words max)

**Slide 15: The Choice (60 seconds)**
- Frame the decision
- Two paths imagery
- "What will you choose?" energy
- 8-12 words

**Slide 16: Call to Action (60-90 seconds)**
- ONE specific action
- Active verb starts the slide
- 6-10 words total
- Compelling visual
- Examples:
  - "Start asking Why before What"
  - "Take 2 minutes before your next meeting"
  - "Share one vulnerable truth today"

**Slide 17: The Future State (30-45 seconds)**
- Beautiful, aspirational image
- 5 words maximum showing transformed world
- Leave them with the vision

**Slide 18: Final Slide - Contact/Next Steps (During Q&A)**
- Clean, minimal design
- Your name
- ONE way to reach you (website or Twitter/LinkedIn)
- NO "Thank You" - say it verbally
- NO "Questions?" - say it verbally
- This slide stays up during Q&A

### Template 2: Steve Jobs Product Launch Format (30-45 minutes)

**Total Slides: 40-60 slides**
**Speaking Pace: 130-150 WPM**
**Total Words: ~3,900-6,750 words**

#### ACT 1: SETUP (8-10 minutes, 1,040-1,500 words)

**Slide 1-2: Pre-Show/Welcome (1-2 min)**
- Minimalist title
- Company logo (if required)
- Ambient music

**Slide 3: The Hook - "Today we're going to..." (30 sec)**
- Just the number "3" or simple text
- ONE thing on screen
- Build anticipation

**Slide 4-6: Context Setting (2-3 min)**
- Where company has been
- Previous innovations
- Build credibility
- ONE image per slide
- Examples: "1984 - Macintosh changed computing"

**Slide 7-9: The Villain/Problem (2-3 min)**
- Show competing products
- Point out weaknesses
- Create the conflict
- Each competitor gets own slide
- Screenshots or product images
- Minimal text annotations

**Slide 10: Our Vision (1 min)**
- What you set out to create
- Aspirational language
- Beautiful imagery
- 5-10 words

#### ACT 2: THE REVEAL & FEATURES (20-25 minutes, 2,600-3,750 words)

**Slide 11-13: The Product Reveal (2-3 min)**
- THREE products that are actually ONE
- Build tension
- Example: "A widescreen iPod with touch controls..."
- Slide for each "product"
- Then reveal they're one device

**Slide 14: Product Hero Shot (1 min)**
- Stunning product photography
- Full bleed image
- ZERO text (or just product name)
- Let them absorb it

**Every 9 Minutes: Change the Pace**
Jobs introduced something new every 9 minutes:
- New speaker
- Video/live demo
- Dramatic story
- Product comparison
- Audience interaction

**Slide 15-35: Features Deep Dive (15-18 min)**

Pattern for each feature:
- **Intro Slide:** Feature name + icon (30 sec)
- **Problem Slide:** What's broken today (45 sec)
- **Solution Slide:** How you solve it (60 sec)
- **Demo Slide:** "Let me show you" (90-120 sec)
  - Often minimal/black slide during live demo
- **Wow Slide:** The magical moment (45 sec)

**Example Feature Sequence (Touch Interface):**
- Slide 15: "Multi-Touch" (just those words)
- Slide 16: Today's keyboards/styluses (image of Blackberry keyboard)
- Slide 17: "What if you could use most natural pointing device?" (image of finger)
- Slide 18: [BLACK - Live Demo Time]
- Slide 19: Zoom in on detail (animated gif or video)

**Repeat this pattern 3-5 times for major features**

**Slide 36: Integration/Ecosystem (2 min)**
- How features work together
- Simple diagram
- Minimal text
- Show the magic of the system

#### ACT 3: SPECS, PRICING, AVAILABILITY (8-10 minutes, 1,040-1,500 words)

**Slide 37-40: Technical Specifications (2-3 min)**
- ONE spec per slide
- Large numbers
- Icons
- Examples:
  - Battery life: "8 hours" dominates slide
  - Storage: "256 GB" with storage icon
  - Screen: "6.7 inches" with device outline

**Slide 41-42: Partnerships (1-2 min)**
- Partner logos (tastefully done)
- Maybe bring partner on stage
- Examples: Google, Yahoo, Carrier

**Slide 43: The Price Anchor (1 min)**
- Anchoring technique
- "You might expect this to cost $999"
- Build anticipation

**Slide 44: The Real Price (1 min)**
- The reveal
- Large number
- Maybe "Starting at $599"
- Let it land

**Slide 45-46: Availability (1-2 min)**
- When
- Where
- How to get it
- Clear, simple timeline

**Slide 47-50: Accessories/Ecosystem (2-3 min)**
- Cases
- Integrations
- Services
- ONE item per slide
- Product photography

#### CLOSING (3-5 minutes, 390-750 words)

**Slide 51-52: Summary (1-2 min)**
- Recap the "three products"
- Or three key innovations
- Callback to opening

**Slide 53: The Vision Restated (1 min)**
- Beautiful product image
- Aspirational text
- 5-8 words
- Example: "The future of mobile computing"

**Slide 54: "One More Thing..." (1-2 min) [OPTIONAL]**
- If you have a surprise
- Build drama
- Just those words on black background
- Then reveal

**Slide 55: Availability Reminder (30 sec)**
- On sale [date]
- Clear call to action

**Slide 56: Thank You Slide (During applause/Q&A)**
- Product image
- Website
- Minimal

#### STEVE JOBS SLIDE DESIGN PRINCIPLES

**Text Rules:**
- Very little text
- Most slides show ONE image
- When he said "The first thing..." slide showed just "1"
- When introducing three features, THREE separate slides, not one slide with three items

**Image Rules:**
- High-quality product photography
- Full-bleed images
- No clip art, no stock photos of handshakes

**Font Rules:**
- One font family (usually Helvetica or Myriad)
- Large sizes (60pt+)
- Generous white space

**Color Rules:**
- Clean palette
- Often black backgrounds for product photos
- White backgrounds for simplicity
- Brand colors used sparingly

**The "One Thing" Rule:**
When introducing feature, don't show one slide with three devices. When speaking about each feature, separate slide appears with image of each feature.

### Template 3: The Data Story Structure (15-20 minutes)

**Total Slides: 12-15 slides**
**For presentations heavy on data, research, or technical content**

**Slide 1: Title (10 sec)**
- Research question or finding
- One compelling data visualization thumbnail

**Slide 2: Why This Research Matters (60 sec)**
- The human impact
- Who cares and why
- Image of people affected (not data yet)

**Slide 3: What We Knew Before (60 sec)**
- Previous understanding
- Maybe one simple chart
- Set up the knowledge gap

**Slide 4: The Question We Asked (45 sec)**
- Your research question
- Simple text
- Maybe question mark visual

**Slide 5: How We Studied It (60-90 sec)**
- Methodology
- Simple diagram (not detailed)
- Build credibility without boring audience
- "We analyzed X participants over Y time"

**Slide 6-10: The Findings (8-10 min)**

**Each finding gets 2 slides:**

**Slide 6a: Finding #1 - The Data (60 sec)**
- ONE chart
- ONE highlighted data point
- Remove everything else
- Use color to direct attention
- 42pt+ labels

**Slide 6b: Finding #1 - What It Means (60 sec)**
- Translation to human terms
- Image or metaphor
- "This means that [human impact]"

**Slide 7a: Finding #2 - The Data (60 sec)**
[Same pattern]

**Slide 7b: Finding #2 - What It Means (60 sec)**
[Same pattern]

**Repeat for 2-3 key findings**

**Slide 11: The Surprise (90 sec)**
- Your most unexpected finding
- Could be STAR moment
- Visualized dramatically
- "We expected X, but found Y"

**Slide 12: What Changes Now (60 sec)**
- Implications
- Bridge from data to action
- Visual metaphor

**Slide 13: The Three Takeaways (90 sec)**
- THREE bullets (exception to no-bullets rule)
- But ONLY three
- Short phrases (5-7 words each)
- Icons or numbers

**Slide 14: Call to Action (60 sec)**
- What should change based on this data
- Specific and measurable
- Visual showing the change

**Slide 15: Contact/Next Steps**
- Your details
- Where to find full research
- Minimal

#### DATA VISUALIZATION BEST PRACTICES

**The Storytelling Arc for Each Chart:**
1. Show full dataset (2 seconds)
2. Highlight the insight (3 seconds)
3. Remove everything else (5+ seconds for audience to absorb)

**Chart Simplification:**
- Remove gridlines
- Remove background colors
- Remove 3D effects
- Remove legends if possible (direct labeling)
- Use ONE accent color for key data point
- Everything else grey

**Text on Data Slides:**
- Chart title: The insight, not just description
  - Bad: "Revenue by Quarter"
  - Good: "Revenue Doubled in Q3"
- Maximum 10 words total on slide
- 42pt+ font size minimum

**Total Slides: 8-12 slides**
**For talks focused on personal journey, lessons learned, transformation**

**Slide 1: Title (10 sec)**
- Evocative image from your story
- Title that hints at journey

**Slide 2: Where I Started (60-90 sec)**
- Image from your "before" state
- Set the scene
- 5-10 words
- Let your voice tell the story

**Slide 3: The Inciting Incident (60-90 sec)**
- What changed everything
- Dramatic image

  - Crisis
  - Realization
  - Opportunity
  - Loss
- Minimal text

**Slide 4: The Struggle (90-120 sec)**
- The hard part
- Image that conveys difficulty

- The moment of change
- Image of breakthrough
- Could be:
  - A person who helped
  - A realization
  - A decision

**Slide 6-8: The Lessons (5-6 min total)**

**Each lesson gets one slide:**

**Pattern:**
- ONE lesson per slide
- ONE image that embodies it
- 8-15 word statement
- Tell the story verbally

**Example:**
- Slide 6: "I learned that vulnerability is not weakness"
  - Image: Open hands or open door

- The transformation

- What they should do
- Based on your lessons
- Specific
- Actionable

**Slide 12: Final Image (30 sec)**
- Aspirational
- Represents transformed state

- Leave them inspired

## PART 4: TIMING & PACING GUIDELINES

### Speaking Pace by Content Type

| Content Type | Words Per Minute | Notes |
|--------------|------------------|--------|
| Emotional storytelling | 120-130 WPM | Slower for impact and pauses |
| Technical explanation | 100-120 WPM | Slower for comprehension |
| Conversational/standard | 125-150 WPM | Natural, comfortable pace |
| High-energy/rallying | 150-175 WPM | Faster for excitement (use sparingly) |
| Data presentation | 110-130 WPM | Slower to let numbers sink in |

### Slide Timing Benchmarks

| Slide Type | Recommended Time | Notes |
|------------|------------------|--------|
| Title slide | 5-15 seconds | Just establish it, move on |
| Hook/opening | 30-60 seconds | Enough to land, not linger |
| Context setting | 45-60 seconds | Set stage efficiently |
| Standard content | 60-90 seconds | Sweet spot for most slides |
| Data visualization | 90-120 seconds | Give time to absorb |
| STAR moment | 60-120 seconds | Let impact build |
| Story slide | 90-180 seconds | Longer OK if compelling |
| Demo/video | 90-180 seconds | Variety in pacing |
| Call to action | 60-90 seconds | Clear and strong |
| Final slide | 30-60 seconds | During closing words |

### The 9-Minute Rule (Steve Jobs)

**Principle:** Every 9 minutes, introduce something to "perk up" the brain and change pace.

**Pace-Changers:**
- New speaker on stage
- Video clip
- Live demonstration
- Audience participation
- Dramatic story
- Unexpected reveal
- Humor break
- Silence/pause

**Example 18-Minute Structure:**
- Minutes 0-9: You presenting (slides + speaking)
- Minute 9: Video or demo (brain perks up)
- Minutes 10-18: You presenting (slides + speaking)
- Minute 18: Dramatic pause or final reveal

**Example 45-Minute Structure:**
- Minutes 0-9: Setup + context
- Minute 9-10: VIDEO #1 (pace change)
- Minutes 10-19: Feature demonstration
- Minute 19-21: GUEST SPEAKER (pace change)
- Minutes 21-30: Technical details
- Minute 30-32: DEMO #2 (pace change)
- Minutes 32-41: Ecosystem + partnerships
- Minute 41-43: "ONE MORE THING" (pace change)
- Minutes 43-45: Closing

### Rehearsal Timing Strategy

**Nancy Duarte's Checkpoint Method:**

For 18-minute talk, establish checkpoints:
- 25% (4.5 min): "I should be on Slide X"
- 50% (9 min): "I should be on Slide Y"
- 75% (13.5 min): "I should be on Slide Z"

**Practice Approach:**
1. Run through full talk with timer
2. Note where you are at checkpoints
3. Adjust content or pacing
4. Repeat until consistent

**Buffer Strategy:**
- Build 10-15% time buffer
- For 18-minute slot, prepare 16-minute talk
- Allows for:
  - Audience laughter
  - Applause
  - Technical delays
  - Natural variation

## PART 5: DESIGN PRINCIPLES MATRIX

### The Core Rules

#### Text Limits

| Authority | Rule | Context |
|-----------|------|---------|
| Nancy Duarte | Maximum 75 words per slide | Beyond this = document, not presentation |
| TED Guidelines | Maximum 25 words per slide | For TED/TEDx talks specifically |
| Garr Reynolds | Aim for zero words | Image-driven, signal over noise |
| 7x7 Rule | 7 lines, 7 words per line max | Traditional guideline (now considered too much) |
| Best Practice | 6-15 words per slide | Modern consensus for impact |

#### Font Specifications

| Element | Size | Type | Notes |
|---------|------|------|--------|
| Body text | 42pt minimum | Sans serif (Helvetica, Verdana) | Readable from back of room |
| Headlines | 60pt+ | Sans serif, bold weight | Command attention |
| Data labels | 42pt minimum | Sans serif | Must be readable in charts |
| Maximum fonts | 2 families | One for headers, one for body | Consistency over variety |

#### Whitespace Requirements

| Source | Guideline | Purpose |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| TED Guidelines | 35%+ whitespace | Breathing room, focus |
| Duarte | 50%+ for high-impact slides | Amplification through simplification |
| Reynolds | Maximum whitespace | Zen minimalism |
| Best Practice | 40-60% whitespace | Balance impact and content |

### Color Strategy (60-30-10 Rule)

**60% - Primary/Background Color**
- Usually white, black, or brand primary
- Establishes overall tone
- Creates consistency

**30% - Secondary Color**
- Supporting color for variety
- Section breaks
- Background variation

**10% - Accent Color**
- For emphasis
- Call-out important data
- Call to action slides
- STAR moments

**Example Palette:**
- 60%: White backgrounds
- 30%: Light grey for alternating slides
- 10%: Bold orange for key data and CTAs

### Image Guidelines

**Quality Standards:**
- Minimum 1920x1080 resolution (for 16:9 slides)
- High-quality photography only
- NO generic stock photos (handshakes, cheesy smiles)
- NO clip art
- Own the rights to all images

**Usage Patterns:**
- Full-bleed images (edge to edge) for emotional impact
- Single focus point per image
- Images that reinforce message, not decorate
- Real photography over illustrations (unless brand-specific)

**Where to Source:**
- Your own photography
- Properly licensed stock (Unsplash, Pexels for free)
- Custom illustrations
- Product photography
- NOT Google Image Search

### Slide Layout Templates

**Layout 1: Full-Bleed Image + Minimal Text**
```

│                             │
│    [FULL BLEED IMAGE]       │
│                             │
│         Short Text          │
│         (6-10 words)        │
│                             │
└─────────────────────────────┘
```
**Use for:** Emotional moments, story beats, section breaks

**Layout 2: Dominant Number/Stat**
```
┌─────────────────────────────┐
│                             │
│                             │
│          71M+               │
│          VIEWS              │
│                             │
│     [small context text]    │
│                             │

```
**Use for:** Statistics, data reveals, STAR moments

**Layout 3: Simplified Chart**
```
┌─────────────────────────────┐
│  Revenue Doubled in Q3      │
│                             │
│    [CLEAN CHART]            │
│    [ONE highlight color]    │
│    [Direct labels, no legend]│
│                             │
└─────────────────────────────┘
```
**Use for:** Data presentation, research findings

**Layout 4: Quote**
```
┌─────────────────────────────┐
│                             │
│                             │
│   "People don't buy what    │
│   you do; they buy why      │
│   you do it."               │
│                             │
```

**Use for:** Expert validation, transition moments

**Layout 5: Icon + Text**
```
┌─────────────────────────────┐
│                             │
│       [LARGE ICON]          │

│                             │
│    [2-3 word descriptor]    │
│                             │
└─────────────────────────────┘
```
**Use for:** Feature introductions, concept explanations

### Aspect Ratio

**Standard: 16:9 (Widescreen)**
- Modern projectors and screens
- TED standard
- Matches video content
- 1920x1080 or 3840x2160 (4K)

**Legacy: 4:3**
- Older conference rooms only
- Avoid if possible

### Animation Guidelines

**When to Use:**
- To reveal information sequentially
- To direct attention to specific data point
- To show process or progression
- To create dramatic reveal

**When NOT to Use:**
- For decoration
- For every slide
- Complex animations that distract
- Transitions between every slide (minimal or none preferred)

**Best Practices:**
- Simple fades or wipes
- Consistent animation style throughout
- Fast animations (0.3-0.5 seconds)
- Purposeful, not playful

## PART 7: QUICK REFERENCE - THE GOLDEN RULES

### The Non-Negotiables

1. **One Idea Per Slide** - If you have multiple ideas, make multiple slides
2. **3-Second Glance Test** - Audience should grasp slide meaning in 3 seconds or less
3. **Audience as Hero** - Make them the protagonist, you're the mentor
4. **Throughline is King** - Every element must support your one core message
5. **Show, Don't Tell** - Images over text, always
6. **Death to Bullet Points** - Exception: final takeaways (max 3 bullets)
7. **Own Your Images** - Rights-managed only, never Google Image Search
8. **STAR Moment Required** - Every presentation needs something they'll always remember
9. **Specific Call to Action** - Not "think about it" but "do this specific thing"
10. **Practice with Timer** - You cannot accurately estimate time without practice

### The Word Limits

- **Slide maximum:** 25 words (TED) / 75 words (business)
- **Throughline:** 15 words maximum
- **Speaking pace:** 125-150 words per minute
- **Slide timing:** 60-90 seconds per slide (standard content)

### The Time Limits

- **Opening hook:** 30-60 seconds to grab attention
- **Context setting:** Under 2 minutes
- **Pace change:** Every 9 minutes
- **STAR moment:** 60-120 seconds
- **Closing:** 3-4 minutes (about 10-15% of total time)
- **Buffer:** Build in 10-15% time cushion

### The Design Limits

- **Font size minimum:** 42pt
- **Font families maximum:** 2
- **Whitespace minimum:** 40%
- **Words per slide:** 6-15 (optimal)
- **Bullets per slide:** 0 (avoid) or 3 maximum (final takeaways only)
- **Colors in palette:** 3 (60-30-10 rule)
- **Ideas per slide:** 1

### The Story Structure

**Setup (10-15% of time):**
- Hook
- Context
- Stakes

**Conflict/Body (70-80% of time):**
- Problem/solution oscillation
- Data/evidence
- STAR moment
- Stories/examples

**Resolution (10-15% of time):**
- The gap
- The choice
- Call to action
- Vision of future

## APPENDIX A: Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Content Mistakes

1. **Too Many Throughlines** - Trying to cover 5-7 concepts instead of 1-3
2. **Burying the Lead** - Saving important reveal for too late
3. **No STAR Moment** - Forgetting something memorable
4. **Vague CTA** - "Think about this" instead of specific action
5. **You as Hero** - Making it about you instead of audience
6. **Starting with Outline** - "Today I'm going to talk about X, Y, and Z"

### Design Mistakes

1. **Text-Heavy Slides** - More than 25 words
2. **Bullet Point Abuse** - Multiple slides of bullets
3. **Tiny Fonts** - Under 42pt
4. **Busy Charts** - Gridlines, legends, 3D effects, multiple colors
5. **Stock Photo Clichés** - Handshakes, cheesy smiles, people pointing at laptops
6. **Low-Resolution Images** - Pixelated photos
7. **Too Many Fonts** - More than 2 families
8. **Cluttered Layouts** - Less than 40% whitespace

### Delivery Mistakes

1. **Reading Slides** - If you're reading, audience is reading, no one is connecting
2. **Ignoring Time** - No checkpoints, running over
3. **Monotone Pace** - Same speed for 18 minutes straight
4. **No Pace Changes** - Talking for full time without breaks/videos/demos
5. **Weak Opening** - Thanking organizers, apologizing, stating outline
6. **Fumbling Closing** - Trailing off, "I guess that's it," weak CTA
7. **"Thank You" Slide** - Last slide says "Thank You" or "Questions?"

### Technical Mistakes

1. **Not Testing Equipment** - Assuming it will work
2. **Streaming Videos** - Not downloaded locally
3. **No Backup** - Single copy of slides on one device
4. **Wrong Aspect Ratio** - 4:3 slides on 16:9 screen (or vice versa)
5. **Fonts Not Embedded** - Slides look different on venue computer
6. **No Offline Mode** - Requiring internet connection

## CONCLUSION

World-class presentations share common DNA:

1. **Clear Throughline** - One powerful idea, expressed in 15 words or less
2. **Audience-Centric** - Make them the hero of the story
3. **Visual Simplicity** - Amplification through simplification, 3-second glance test
4. **Emotional Connection** - 65% pathos, storytelling over data dumps
5. **Memorable Moments** - STAR moments that create emotionally charged events
6. **Strategic Pacing** - Pace changes every 9 minutes, checkpoint timing
7. **Specific CTA** - Clear, actionable next step

**The Formula:**
- Setup (10-15%): Hook + Context + Stakes
- Conflict (70-80%): Problem/Solution Oscillation + STAR Moment + Evidence
- Resolution (10-15%): Gap + Choice + CTA + Vision

**The Design:**
- 1 idea per slide
- 6-25 words per slide
- 42pt+ fonts
- 40%+ whitespace
- High-quality images only
- 60-30-10 color rule

**The Delivery:**
- 125-150 WPM speaking pace
- 60-90 seconds per slide
- First 30 seconds = hook
- Last 3-4 minutes = CTA + vision
- Practice with timer

Now you have the frameworks. Now you have the structures. Now you have the principles.

The rest is practice, iteration, and courage to tell your story.
